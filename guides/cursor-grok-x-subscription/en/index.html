Cursor, Grok and X: Which subscription should you choose?

Confused by the old and new plans, shared allowances and migration options? Before paying, here's a simple way to choose, organized around seven common use cases.

New subscriptions:
How to choose?

Cursor, Grok and X subscriptions have recently been brought together under The X Subscription. At first glance, paying for everything together seems more convenient. But there are three details that are easy to miss: a shared usage pool, extra charges for third-party models, and the fact that merging legacy subscriptions cannot be undone.

This guide reflects the official information available on October 17, 2026. Prices and benefits change quickly; check the actual checkout page before paying. This is an independent usage guide, not official purchasing advice.

The annual price is the amount paid for a whole year up front, not a monthly subscription you can cancel whenever you like. Grok Bot is included starting with Premium. Under the new plans, Grok and Cursor share a weekly usage allowance.

Choose based on these seven scenarios

  1. You just want to explore the new features and aren't sure you'll use them. Try Free first. Rather than paying immediately because a bundle looks like good value, figure out which tools you'll actually use.
  2. You use AI occasionally and are on a budget. Consider Plus ($8/month), bearing in mind that it does not include Grok Bot. Light AI use and an automation agent are different needs.
  3. You want to use Grok regularly and also need Grok Bot. Start by comparing Premium ($30/month), since Plus does not include Grok Bot. This tier is also a reasonable starting point to investigate for many moderate users.
  4. You code frequently with Cursor but primarily use Claude or GPT. Don't just compare subscription prices. Third-party model usage in Cursor is charged separately under the new plans, which can make an apparently inexpensive plan more costly.
  5. You make heavy use of Grok and Cursor's own models. Compare Super ($100/month) and the size of its usage pool. Upgrading makes sense only once you're sure Premium's allowance isn't enough.
  6. You work intensively every day and consistently consume a large amount of usage. That's when Ultra ($200/month) is worth evaluating carefully. More expensive doesn't automatically mean better; this plan is for people who know they need the extra capacity.
  7. You already have a legacy Cursor, SuperGrok or X subscription. Don't rush to merge. Eligible legacy subscriptions can be kept. Switching cancels the selected old plans, and merging is irreversible. Do the math first, especially if you have discounted pricing, legacy benefits or App Store billing.

Three pitfalls to check before paying

01 · Shared usage does not mean a separate allowance for every product

Eligible Grok and Cursor usage under the new plans comes from a shared pool that refreshes weekly. Grok Bot draws from the same pool. You can't simply add together the allowances displayed for each product.

02 · Third-party models in Cursor are not a free bonus

Under the new plans, third-party models such as Claude and GPT in Cursor are not included in the built-in usage allowance. From the first use, they may be billed at API rates against prepaid credits. If you rely mainly on these models, this is the most important detail to verify before buying.

03 · Existing subscribers should check their billing before switching

Eligible legacy subscriptions can be retained; moving to a unified subscription is not mandatory. The selected legacy subscriptions are canceled when switching, and unused portions are generally refunded on a prorated basis. However, Cursor subscriptions paid through the Apple App Store are not automatically canceled and must be managed in Apple subscriptions. Merging is irreversible, so verify any existing discounts and benefits first.

My quick recommendation

If you're just curious, try it first. If you actually need Grok Bot, begin by evaluating Premium. If you mostly use Cursor with Claude or GPT, focus on the extra model charges. And if you already have a legacy plan that offers good value, don't rush into migrating.

A unified subscription isn't necessarily a better deal. Start with what you actually use, then decide how much you're willing to spend each month.
